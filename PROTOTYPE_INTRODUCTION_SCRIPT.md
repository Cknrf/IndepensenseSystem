# IndepenSense Prototype — Introduction Script

**Use this script to introduce the system to a new test user. Read it aloud in a calm, clear voice.**

---

## Opening (1-2 minutes)

"Hello! Thank you for helping us test IndepenSense, a new navigation and safety system designed for blind and visually impaired people.

Today you'll be wearing a wearable device about the size of a smartphone. It's equipped with:
- Sensors on your cane that detect obstacles
- Vibration motors that give you tactile feedback
- A speaker for voice guidance
- Buttons for controlling the system

The system has three main jobs:
1. **Obstacle detection** — tells you when something is ahead while you're walking
2. **Voice navigation** — guides you turn-by-turn to any location you ask for
3. **Safety** — detects falls and can alert your emergency contacts

I'm going to walk you through everything step by step, and we'll practice in this safe, familiar area before you try it on a real walk. Does that sound good?"

---

## Finding the Buttons (2-3 minutes)

"Let me show you the three buttons on the device. I'll guide your hand to each one.

**First button: The Silver button — this is the PTT button (Push-to-Talk)**

*Guide their hand to the button.*

"This button is how you talk to the system. Here's how it works:
- You HOLD DOWN this button
- While holding, you speak your command
- Then you RELEASE the button
- The system listens and responds

For example, you'd say 'Navigate me to the nearest coffee shop' and then release.

Let's practice. Hold down this button and say 'Hello system' and then release."

*Wait for them to practice.*

"Great! You did it perfectly. That's how voice commands work.

**Second button: The Red button — this is the Emergency button**

*Guide their hand to the button.*

"This is only for emergencies. To activate it, you need to PRESS AND HOLD for at least 2 seconds. When you do:
- An alarm will sound
- All the vibration motors will activate
- Your emergency contacts will get a text with your location

We WON'T practice this one today — it's a real emergency alert — but it's good to know where it is.

If you accidentally press it, you have 10 seconds to press it again to cancel.

**Third button: The Blue button — this is the Repeat button**

*Guide their hand to the button.*

"This button has three uses:
1. Press it to repeat the last thing the system said
2. Press it to stop the system while it's speaking
3. Press it to cancel a voice command you're recording

So if you miss something, just press this blue button and it'll repeat it.

Let's make sure you can find all three buttons quickly. Without looking, can you point to each one?"

*Have them locate each button a few times without guidance.*

---

## Vibration Feedback (2-3 minutes)

"Now let me explain the vibrations you're about to feel. The device has three small motors that vibrate in different ways to tell you different things.

**Most important: Obstacle Feedback**

As you walk forward with this device on, it's constantly scanning for obstacles on your cane — things like walls, curbs, or doors.

When an obstacle is detected ahead, you'll feel vibration that:
- Starts slow and subtle when the obstacle is far away (about 6 meters)
- Gets progressively faster and stronger as you get closer
- Reaches very fast intense vibration when you're about to hit it

Think of it like echolocation feedback — the vibration pattern directly shows you the distance.

Here's the key: **This only works while you're walking.** If you stand still, you won't feel obstacle vibrations. This is intentional — we don't want to distract you when you're not moving.

Let me give you a quick taste of what this feels like. I'm going to turn on a light vibration for about 3 seconds so you know what to expect."

*Activate a test vibration pattern.*

"That's what you'll feel. On your next walk, when you feel that pattern getting stronger, it means something is ahead and you're getting closer.

**Other vibration patterns:**

There are a few other vibration types:
- Sharp pulses on all motors together = navigation turn coming up, or confirmation signal
- Rapid pulses on all motors = fall detected, or emergency alert
- Very slow pulse = battery is low

Any questions about the vibrations before we move on?"

---

## Voice Navigation (2-3 minutes)

"Now let's talk about navigation. This is how you get from one place to another.

**How to start navigation:**

Hold down the silver PTT button and say something like:
- 'Navigate me to the nearest Starbucks'
- 'Navigate me to work'
- 'Navigate me to the subway station'

Then release the button. The system will:
1. Find that location on maps
2. Show you where it is
3. Ask you to confirm
4. Give you turn-by-turn voice guidance

**What you'll hear during navigation:**

- 'Turn left in 50 meters' — advance notice
- 'Turn left now' — time to turn
- 'Continue straight' — keep going same direction
- 'Destination reached' — you've arrived

**Important:** While navigating, the obstacle feedback still works. So you'll hear turn instructions AND feel vibrations if something is in your path. The system is smart about balancing both.

If you miss something, just press the blue Repeat button and the system will say it again.

Does this make sense? Any questions about how navigation works?"

---

## Fall Detection & Safety (1-2 minutes)

"This device also has built-in safety features.

**Fall Detection:**

The device has a motion sensor that can tell if you've fallen. If it detects a fall, it will:
1. Alert you by saying 'Fall detected'
2. Give you 10 seconds to respond
3. Ask if you need help

If you press the emergency button, your contacts get alerted. If you don't need help, just let the alert go away — no action needed.

Sometimes it might false-alarm if you sit down suddenly or make a quick motion. That's normal — just ignore it if you're fine.

**Emergency Alert:**

If you ever feel unsafe or need immediate help, press the red emergency button for 2+ seconds. Your emergency contacts will get a text message with your GPS location. This is for real emergencies only.

**Battery:**

The device will tell you when the battery is low. Make sure to charge it after each session. I'll handle that today — don't worry about it.

Any questions about safety features?"

---

## Final Checklist Before Walking (1 minute)

"Before you take your first walk, let me make sure everything is clear:

- Can you find the silver PTT button? Go ahead and press it now.
- Can you find the blue Repeat button? Perfect.
- Do you understand that obstacle vibrations only work while walking?
- Do you know that you'll hear voice guidance for turns?
- Do you have any questions about the emergency button?

Great! I think you're ready for your first walk. We're going to start in this familiar area, and I'll be right here with you the whole time.

Any final questions before we get started?"

---

## During the Walk (Ongoing coaching)

**Stay nearby and provide encouragement:**

"You're doing great! That vibration you felt? That was an obstacle detection. You're about 100 meters away from the location. The system will guide you in."

"Perfect turn! You're exactly where the system told you to go."

"That vibration ramped up — you're getting closer to something. Should we slow down?"

**Let them navigate, but prompt if needed:**

"Do you remember what button to press if you want to hear the turn instruction again?"

"That's the obstacle vibration — the pattern tells you how close it is."

---

## Wrapping Up (5 minutes)

At the end of the session:

"That was excellent! You handled everything really well. Let me ask you a few questions about your experience:

1. **How did the obstacle feedback feel?** Did the vibration pattern make sense to you?
2. **How was the navigation guidance?** Were the turn instructions clear?
3. **Did anything confuse you or feel difficult?**
4. **What surprised you (in a good or bad way)?**
5. **If you could change one thing about the system, what would it be?**

[Listen to their feedback and take notes]

Thank you so much for testing IndepenSense today. Your feedback is incredibly valuable in helping us build better tools for the blind and visually impaired community."

---

## Tips for Researchers

- **Stay calm and encouraging** — this is a new system; some confusion is normal
- **Don't over-explain** — let them experience the feedback themselves
- **Watch their body language** — are they comfortable and confident?
- **Take notes on their reactions** — note what surprised them, what confused them
- **Encourage questions** — if they're quiet, they might be processing; check in: "How's it feeling?"
- **Let them lead** — after the introduction, let them try things; you're observing, not driving
- **If something breaks** — stay calm, acknowledge it, move on; this is why we test

**Good luck with your testing session!** 🚀
